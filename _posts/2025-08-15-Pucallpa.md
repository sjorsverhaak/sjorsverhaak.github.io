    When you drive towards Peru&#39;s Boiling River, along a bumpy, four-hour track through the rainforest, it&#39;s only after you transcend a crest in the landscape that you see it ahead of you, says Alyssa Kullberg, a postdoctoral researcher in plant ecology at the Swiss Federal Technology Institute of Lausanne (EPFL). Huge billows of steam rise from a clutch of trees in the wide, saucer-like depression below.<br><br>
    &quot;It was so magical,&quot; says Kullberg, remembering the first time she saw it with her own eyes. The Boiling River, also known as the Shanay-timpishka, is part of a tributary in east-central Peru that connects to the mighty Amazon River.&rdquo;
